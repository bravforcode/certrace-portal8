# Certrace Design System — Executive Summary

*For founders, marketers, and business stakeholders. No design background
needed.*

## The design system in plain English

Certrace looks like **a beautifully printed document that came alive**.
Everything sits on a warm paper-colored background — never pure white, never
dark. Words are set in large, thin, elegant type. Small typewriter-style
labels organize the page. Instead of photos or illustrations, the brand uses
its own data: live tables, ticking counters, and little pulsing green dots
that show the platform working in real time.

Color is used the way an auditor uses a highlighter: green means verified,
amber means pending, red means overdue — and that's it. There is no "brand
blue." The restraint *is* the brand.

## Why it works

- **It matches the promise.** Certrace sells calm and order in a chaotic,
  high-stakes domain. A quiet, precise interface makes that promise
  believable before a single word is read.
- **It shows, never tells.** Live supplier tables and audit trails act as
  proof. Competitors say "trusted by"; Certrace looks operational.
- **It's enterprise-credible.** Thin type, hairline lines, and zero gimmicks
  read as infrastructure — the aesthetic of companies that handle serious
  money and regulation.
- **It's cheap to maintain.** No photoshoots, no illustration budget. The
  visuals are generated by code and by typography.

## What makes it unique

Most SaaS pages are white-and-blue with stock 3D blobs. Certrace is warm
paper, ink, typewriter labels, pixel-art glyphs, and living data. You can
recognize a Certrace screen from across the room with the logo covered —
that's the test of a real identity.

## How to keep it consistent

Three rules cover 90% of cases:

1. **Always the paper background** (`#F5F4F0`) — slides, posts, PDFs, pages.
2. **Big thin headlines, tiny capital labels** — never bold titles, never
   colorful text.
3. **Color only as status** — small green/amber/red dots and chips, nothing
   else.

If something feels "loud," it's off-brand. When in doubt, remove color, add
whitespace.

## Using it day-to-day

- **Presentations & Canva:** start every slide on the paper background; one
  big light headline per slide; use the wordmark `CERTRACE` in the typewriter
  font; follow the recipe in [BRAND_GUIDELINES.md](./BRAND_GUIDELINES.md) §4.
- **Marketing materials:** lead with a stat (e.g., "94%") in huge thin type;
  one message per asset; finish with the dark "REQUEST A DEMO" chip.
- **New pages & products:** hand developers/AI tools
  [DESIGN_SYSTEM_AI_CONTEXT.md](./DESIGN_SYSTEM_AI_CONTEXT.md) and
  [design-tokens.json](./design-tokens.json) — they contain everything needed
  to produce matching screens automatically.
- **Quality check:** compare any new asset against the landing page. Same
  paper? Same thin headline? Color only on small status elements? Ship it.
